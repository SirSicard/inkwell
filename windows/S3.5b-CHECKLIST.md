# S3.5b checklist: meetings end to end on the desktop

What an SSH session cannot check: real calls, the Drop on screen and its buttons, taking the
microphone away mid-call, killing the app mid-meeting, and a stretch of everyday use. About an hour
at the PC, signed in, for sections 1 to 8; then the ten calls (section 9) and the dogfood (section
10) over the days after. Note the date, the Windows build and the commit under Result.

Automated checks cover the logic behind each line:

- `cargo test -p ink-ffi --test meetings_win` (in `core`): an app offered by its executable, the
  user's answer, the far end planned as on Windows (Teams on its output device, said as
  "everything"; Zoom alone; a Zoom that is gone falls back to the default output and says so),
  Stop, and the final pass writing the record with one far voice and no summary, said. Replay
  files stand in for the devices.
- `cargo test -p ink-ffi --lib capture::` and `runtime::` on Windows: the mic's reason words, the
  watchdog's routing (a Bluetooth mic's zeros are its user's silence), and the real mic and
  loopback opening through the core (`-- --ignored`, runs over SSH).
- `cargo test -p ink-ffi --test crash_recovery`: a core killed outright mid-meeting (a real
  `TerminateProcess`) loses at most one chunk, and the next launch finishes the meeting.
- `cargo test -p ink-ffi --test device_change`, and `meetings_win`'s follow test: a far end
  whose output changes is opened again and goes on in the same recording; one that ends by
  itself is said with Windows' reason; one that cannot be opened again turns to "stopped" within
  the watchdog's limit and comes back when it can. `ink-platform-win`'s `far_end_moved` test: when
  the far end moves (its output went, the default changed under Record now, Teams plays
  elsewhere) and when it stays.
- `dotnet test` (in `windows`): `MeetingDropTests` (what the Drop says and when, for a meeting and
  the offer), `DropAnswerTests` (the buttons' commands and their failures), `DropTests` (the
  panel's buttons and clicks, the plain fallback's too).

## Setup

As in `S3.5a-CHECKLIST.md`: in a **Developer Command Prompt for VS** (x64), build the core with
its engines and publish the app, then stage the models (Qwen3-ASR for the final pass, Silero for
voice detection) with `scripts/stage-dictation-models.sh`. Start it on a scratch library:

```
set INK_DATA_DIR=%TEMP%\inkwell-meetings
set INK_MODELS_DIR=<models folder>
%TEMP%\inkwell-app\Inkwell.exe
```

Have a second device (a phone) to join your test calls as the other side, and speak from both.

- [ ] Settings > Meetings: "Offer to record calls" is on.
- [ ] Settings > AI: meetings say "No language model is available on this PC, so meetings get no
      summary." (this build has no language model on Windows).

## 1. The offer (detection and consent)

Start a Teams call (new Teams) with the phone.

- [ ] Within about 5 s of Teams opening the microphone, the Drop rises at the bottom centre:
      "Microsoft Teams opened the microphone" over "Recording keeps both sides on this PC. Tell
      the others you are recording.", with **Record this call** (ink) and **Not this one**. The
      ink is still. If it names something else (`msedgewebview2`, say), write down what.
- [ ] While it shows, type in Teams' chat box: every key goes to Teams. Click on the Drop's
      paper (not a button): Teams stays the active window.
- [ ] **Not this one**: the Drop goes, and does not come back while this call goes on.
- [ ] Hang up and call again: the offer comes back. **Record this call**.
- [ ] Discord in a voice channel, NVIDIA Broadcast, Voice Access or the Copilot app's wake word
      (whichever you have) never raise an offer.

## 2. Recording

- [ ] The Drop reads "● REC · Microsoft Teams" (red) over "Recording this meeting"; the ink moves,
      each drop with its own side (yours with your voice, theirs with the phone's).
- [ ] The window's navigation lists **Live**. Live's header: "Recording · 0:12" counting, and
      "Recording everything this PC plays, as well as your microphone." (Teams is recorded from
      its output device, S0.4). Its mic line names your mic.
- [ ] Live's transcript stays "Waiting for someone to speak." (no live words on Windows yet).
- [ ] Ask a question in Live: it says no language model is available to answer on this PC.
- [ ] Windows' microphone-in-use icon shows Teams and Inkwell.

## 3. Stop, the final pass and the record

- [ ] After two minutes or so, Live's **Stop** (or Ctrl+.). The Drop says "Blotting · final pass"
      over "Microsoft Teams"; Live's header "Blotting…". Within about 30 s the Drop goes and Live
      leaves the navigation.
- [ ] Library: the meeting is at the top. Open it: what you said reads as "You", what the phone
      said as "Them" (one voice for the far end: no speaker labels on Windows yet). The words are
      right, both sides, in order.
- [ ] It has no summary, and says so ("Summaries are off until you allow them in Settings >
      AI", where Settings > AI says no language model is available on this PC); nothing is made
      up. (The Mac says the same without a model.)
- [ ] Play it from the record: both sides are heard, in step.

## 4. A call that ends by itself

- [ ] Record a Teams call from the offer, then hang up in Teams (don't press Stop). About 15 s
      later the meeting stops by itself and blots. A mute in Teams in the middle does not end it.

## 5. Record now, Zoom and the browsers

- [ ] With nothing offered, Today's **Record now**: Live says "Recording everything this PC plays,
      as well as your microphone."; a YouTube video playing in a browser is in the record.
- [ ] A Zoom call: offered as "Zoom opened the microphone"; recorded; Live says nothing about
      "everything" (Zoom is heard alone); a YouTube video playing at the same time is **not** in
      the record.
- [ ] A Google Meet call in Chrome, then one in Edge: offered as "Chrome ..." / "Microsoft Edge
      ..."; recorded with both sides; the other browser's sound is not in the record.

## 6. The watchdog: nothing recorded in silence without saying so

- [ ] During a recorded call, Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone: switch off "Let desktop
      apps access your microphone". Within 20 s, Live says "Your microphone is silent" or "Your
      microphone stopped", and Today says "Inkwell can't hear you in this call". Switch it back
      on. Note which, and how many seconds it took.
- [ ] During a recorded call, mute the PC's sound (the speaker icon). Within 20 s the Drop turns
      to "The other side is silent" (red border) and Today says "Inkwell can't hear the other side
      of this call". Unmute: the Drop goes back to "● REC". Note the seconds.
- [ ] A quiet stretch: in a recorded call, nobody speaks for 30 s (both muted in the call, the PC
      not muted). Record whether the Drop turns red. (If it does, the call app plays exact
      silence while nobody talks: a false alarm to fix, P1.)
- [ ] With a USB mic recorded, unplug it mid-call: within 20 s Live says "Your microphone
      stopped". The rest of the call is still recorded from the far end.

## 6b. The far end follows the call's output

Teams (and every app but Zoom and the browsers) is recorded from the output it plays to, so the
recording has to move when the call does.

- [ ] A recorded Teams call on the PC's speakers: plug in a headset (or pick it as Teams'
      speaker). Keep talking from the phone. The Drop stays "● REC" (nothing turns red). In the
      record afterwards, the phone's side goes on across the switch; note any gap you hear.
- [ ] Unplug the headset (Teams goes back to the speakers): the same.
- [ ] Record now while a video plays, then pick another output in the taskbar's sound menu:
      the video is still in the record after the change.
- [ ] A call whose only output is a USB or Bluetooth device: unplug it mid-call. Within 20 s the
      Drop says "The other side stopped". Plug it back in: within about 5 s the Drop is back to
      "● REC", and the record has the other side again from then on.

## 7. Crash recovery (kill -9)

- [ ] Record a call for at least two minutes, speaking from both sides. From a terminal:
      `taskkill /F /IM Inkwell.exe`. The Drop goes at once (a killed app's tray icon can linger
      until the pointer passes over it).
- [ ] Start Inkwell again (same `INK_DATA_DIR`). Within a minute Today says "A meeting was
      finished after Inkwell quit unexpectedly", and the Library has the meeting, with both sides
      up to within 10 seconds of the kill (at most one chunk lost).
- [ ] Quit and start once more: nothing is recovered twice.

## 8. The offer's edges

- [ ] An offer shows, and you start dictating (hold right Ctrl): the Drop dictates; let go: the
      offer is back.
- [ ] **Record this call** while no microphone is plugged in (or with every input disabled): the
      Drop says "Couldn't start recording: ..." in red, and the buttons stay to try again.
- [ ] Display scaling at 150 % (Settings > Display): the offer's buttons are where they are drawn
      and answer clicks there.

## 10. The dogfood

Use Inkwell for Windows' meetings for a week. Keep the log with your own records, not in this
repository (it names real calls). Exit: no open P0 bug.

## Known limits (not failures)

- No live words during a meeting yet: Windows has no live-partials engine in this build, so the
  transcript arrives with the final pass.
- The far end is one voice ("Them"): the diarizer is not built for Windows yet.
- No summary, commitments or Ask: no language model on Windows yet; each says so.
- Teams (and any app but Zoom and the browsers) is recorded from the output device it plays to,
  so other sounds that device plays are in the record; Live says so.
- The far end follows the call's output (section 6b), asked about every 2 s: a second or two of
  the other side can be missing at each switch. A switched **mic** is not followed: that side
  stops, and the watchdog says so, as on the Mac.
- Where an app plays is read from its audio sessions by executable name. An app that plays
  through a helper process with another name is taken to play on the default output, so its far
  end follows the default output.
- A far end whose capture fails to start (Zoom quitting between the offer and the answer) is
  said as "The other side stopped" within about 10 s, not followed.
- Detection names the executable that holds the microphone; an app whose helper process holds it
  is offered by the helper's name.
- The Drop's buttons answer the mouse only (it never takes focus); a screen reader's reach to
  them comes with the Drop's accessibility work.
- The calendar is not read in this unpackaged build: a meeting is untitled until its summary
  would name it, so it is named by its app.
